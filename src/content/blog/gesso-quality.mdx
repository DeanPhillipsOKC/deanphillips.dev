This is part 4 of a series about Gesso, an ongoing experiment in turning any picture into a painting lesson. This part covers day one. The [overview](/blog/gesso/) has the finished lessons to flip through.

## Four settings, one lesson

I made the backlit trees lesson four times, once at each quality setting, and timed each one from start to finish.

## What I saw

- **Max** has the richest paint texture and the finest branches. It also took four times as long as low.
- **High** is nearly as good, except its sky came out in blue stripes.
- **Medium** is clean and consistent, and barely slower than low.
- **Low** is softer and drops some of the thinnest twigs, but it's easy to follow.

The surprise was high's stripy sky. That isn't the setting's fault. Each tab is a separate run, and two runs at the *same* setting differ about as much as these four do. The dial matters less than luck.

## Where the dial ended up

What I care about while experimenting is whether a lesson follows directions: right order, nothing missing, every step the same painting. All four settings did that equally well. So I run experiments at **low**, and I only turn it up if a run stops following directions.

There's one exception. Since the [drift fixes](/blog/gesso-drift/), every step copies the finished painting. A better finished painting makes every step better. So the finished painting gets made at **high**, and the steps stay at low.

That's the series so far. Gesso is still an experiment, and the next test is the one that counts: a beginner, a brush, and one of these lessons. Back to the [overview](/blog/gesso/).
