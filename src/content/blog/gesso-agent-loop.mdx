## The ladder

The agent is Claude, running inside Claude Code, following a long instruction file I wrote (Claude Code calls it a skill). The thinking it does costs nothing extra on my subscription. The pictures cost real money, a couple of cents each from an image model.

So the most important rule in the file is a ladder. For every picture the lesson needs, try the tools in this order, and only go down a rung when the one above can't do it:

1. **A filter.** Free and instant, like the ones in part 5.
2. **A throwaway script.** Claude writes a small one-off program for this picture, two tries at most.
3. **A seeded render.** The image model, starting from the best free attempt.
4. **A plain render.** The image model, from scratch, with a written reason why nothing above worked.

Here's the real run, replayed from its log. Every picture drops in at the top and falls until something can make it.

<ToolLadder jobs={day2.jobs} caption="The balloon lesson's real run, picture by picture. Free rungs are green, paid rungs are purple. The counter is what the images cost." />

The planning pictures all stopped on the first rung for free: the simplified reference, the value study, and the palette. Every lesson step fell to the bottom. That's not the agent being lazy, and the reason it wrote down is a good one: each step has to show something a later layer covers. The sky runs behind the hills. The lavender rows sit under the flowers. No filter can show what's hiding under paint that's already there. Building that tool is next on my list, and it should move most of those steps up the ladder.

## The checks

## The part I didn't tell it to do

A finished step picture shows the shape, not how to get there. When I tried an earlier lesson, a pumpkin patch, I noticed every pumpkin appeared fully formed, with no hint of how to start one. The real tutorial I'd learned it from starts with an oval and adds curved pieces on each side, and that's what made it click for me.

I could have fixed the pumpkin and moved on. Instead I asked for the general version: look at every step like someone who has never painted, decide where the picture isn't enough, and draw a diagram of how to build the shape from simple pieces. The diagrams are drawn as code, not as AI images, so they're free.

On the balloon lesson, nobody told it where to look. It drew diagrams for five of the eleven steps on its own. Here's the balloon:

It also wrote down why the other six steps didn't need one. The clouds, for example: "a texture made by repeating one dabbing mark." That record matters as much as the drawings. It's how I can tell it actually looked at every step instead of drawing a balloon because balloons are obvious.
