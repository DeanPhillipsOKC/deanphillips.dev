Here's the whole idea in one box. Pick a picture, then press Play, or drag the slider, and watch it get painted one step at a time, starting from a blank canvas. Each step comes with the paints to mix for it.

<Flipbook sequences={seqs} caption="Four lessons Gesso made. Every step is a picture of the canvas after that step, with the colors you mix for it." />

No person painted any of these steps. Every painting is AI-generated, and every lesson was planned, painted, checked, and written by AI models following rules I set up. It took one day, from an empty folder at 2:21 in the afternoon to these four lessons that evening. Along the way it made 30 lessons, and a lot of the early ones were wrong in ways that are fun to look at.

## How it works

The trick is that Gesso paints the lesson **backward**. It's like a cooking show pulling the finished cake out of the oven before anyone cracks an egg. Once you know exactly what the finished painting looks like, you can work out what the canvas looked like halfway, and at the start.

## The series

Each part is one thing I learned, with the pictures to prove it:

1. [Paint the sky first](/blog/gesso-sky-first/): the first lessons painted the trees before the sky. One question from me fixed it.
2. [Steps that fit the picture](/blog/gesso-planned-steps/): why a fixed list of steps made boring lessons, and what happened when Claude planned the steps for each picture instead.
3. [Keeping every step the same painting](/blog/gesso-drift/): an image model repaints the whole picture every time, so things wander. How to keep a lesson from drifting.
4. [How good do the pictures need to be?](/blog/gesso-quality/): the same lesson at four quality settings, side by side.

<Callout title="Who did what.">I set the goal, picked the painting style, wrote the rules the AI follows, and made every call about what was right and wrong. Claude (an AI model) planned the lessons and wrote the code with me, and an image model painted every picture. The next test is the real one: my partner and I following one of these with actual brushes.</Callout>
