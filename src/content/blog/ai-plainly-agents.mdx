This is part 3 of AI, plainly. It builds on [part 1's inference](/blog/ai-plainly/) and [part 2's tools](/blog/ai-plainly-tools/).

Say you type one line. "Book me two tickets to the outdoor concert in Tulsa on a weekend day when it isn't raining." A few turns later you have two seats on a dry day, and all you typed after that was yes.

Here's where my kid-level explanation ran out. Part 2 got the model one request and one result. This job takes trying something, checking, and trying again. So how does it do that?

Agents get sold as a smarter kind of AI that keeps thinking in the background. The picture below is what's really there.

## A loop around the loop

<AgentLoop caption="One trip around the loop is a turn. Each trip runs inference once, with everything so far." />

An **agent** is inference run in a loop. The model runs, the harness does whatever it asked for, and the model runs again with the result added. It keeps going until the model stops asking for things, or the harness stops it. The program running the loop is the **harness** (part 2 called it the app). The ChatGPT app is one. When it searches the web, runs code, or works through a whole task in its agent mode, it's running this loop.

Notice what “done” means. The model thinks it's finished. Nothing in the loop checks that the job really got done. More on that at the end.

One trip around is a **turn**. People call this the agentic loop. (Sometimes the model asks for several tools at once. The harness runs them all and pastes every result in before calling it again.)

## One run, turn by turn

Here's the whole job as texts. Every turn, the app opens a brand new chat with the model and pastes in everything so far, plus the newest result.

<TextThreads
  threads={d.threads}
  caption="You only see your own chats. Everything in between happens out of sight. The concert, weather, and prices are made up. Real jobs often run many more turns."
/>

Turn 3 is the one to watch. Nothing in the job said to check Sunday. A script only tries the days and fallbacks someone wrote ahead of time, and nobody wrote this one. The model read the rain and picked the next thing to try. It does it again in turn 5, after “sold out.”

It stops one of two ways. Either it writes an answer with no tool request, so there's nothing left for the harness to do, or the harness stops it (too many turns, too much time or money, or it needs your OK first). The OK kind is in there too. The app paused before spending your money, and the run went on once you said yes.

## Why checks matter

A loop that only does things will happily do the wrong thing. That's part 1's confident guesser, now taking actions. Remember what “done” means. The model stopped asking for things. Nothing checked the result.

So someone has to build a check in. It can be ordinary code, like making sure the receipt says a weekend day and two seats. Or it can be one more new chat with the model that gets only the job and the result, and is asked to find problems.

More loops don't check anything by themselves. Someone has to build the check in.

## What's next

Long jobs have a catch. The conversation keeps growing, and there's a limit to how much of it the model can read at once. What the harness does about that, and why agents get forgetful and flaky on long jobs, gets its own post.

First, though, every word the agent writes comes out of the same dials, set during training. That goes for its thinking, its tool requests and its answers alike. Nobody set them by hand. So how does nudging them a little at a time ever land on the right settings? It's math, but nothing past arithmetic. That's the next post.
