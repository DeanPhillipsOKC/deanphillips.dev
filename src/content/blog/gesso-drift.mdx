This is part 3 of a series about Gesso, an ongoing experiment in turning any picture into a painting lesson. This part covers day one. The [overview](/blog/gesso/) has the finished lessons to flip through.

## Same painting, three ways

Picture asking a painter to repaint your painting from memory at every stage, and to make each copy look a little less finished than the last. The clouds would move. The road would bend somewhere new. That's what an image model does, so I tried three ways of keeping it honest.

- **Backward:** paint the finished picture first, then make each earlier step by taking paint off the step after it.
- **Forward:** start with a blank canvas and add paint each step, aiming at the finished picture.
- **Backward, checking the finish:** like backward, but every step also gets the finished painting to copy shapes from.

Pick a step, then switch tabs. The aurora road is a good test because the road has a very specific shape.

Forward drifted the most. Rows of trees appeared and disappeared, the road changed shape, and the last step looked nothing like the one before it. Backward was better, but the road was a simple striped shape for two steps and then suddenly became the real road. Checking the finish kept everything lined up from start to finish.

Then I tried one more: make every step straight from the finished painting, all at the same time. It came out just as consistent, and since nothing waits on anything else, all the steps get painted together in about 30 seconds instead of about two minutes. That's what Gesso uses now.

## An AI teacher that checks the work

Better methods still let mistakes through, so now there's a second pair of eyes. When all the steps are done, Claude looks at the whole lesson in order, like a teacher grading homework, and flags anything a beginner following along would notice:

- something showing up before the step that paints it
- something from an earlier step that's missing or moved
- a step that doesn't match its own instructions

Each flagged step gets painted again with a note saying what to fix. It caught real problems right away. In one aurora lesson, the green aurora and the moon disappeared from four middle steps and then came back for the finish. In a trees lesson, the trunks were in different places than in the finished painting.

<Callout title="Fixing the cause, not just the symptom.">The disappearing aurora happened every time, so I changed the instructions each step gets. Now every step lists everything that's already been painted and everything that hasn't. The next run needed one fix instead of four (the moon had lost its starburst), and the trees needed none.</Callout>

## What broke

The checker compares each step against the finished painting. Nothing compares the finished painting against your picture. So when the cat came out wrong, every step agreed with it perfectly:
