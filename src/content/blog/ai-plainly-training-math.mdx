This is part 4 of AI, plainly. New to the series? Start with [part 1](/blog/ai-plainly/). [Part 3](/blog/ai-plainly-agents/) ended on a loose end. Every turn of an agent runs through the same dials, set during training. So how do they get set?

In part 1 you turned the dials yourself. Training is a computer doing exactly that, with nobody watching and billions of dials. Part 1 said every wrong guess nudges them. When I tried to explain what a nudge is, I got stuck. So this post follows one guess from start to finish, with nothing past arithmetic.

Every dial is a slope, and training's whole job is setting the slopes. Here's how it turns them.

## The nudge

After a guess, go answer by answer. The right answer should have been 100%. The others should have been 0%. The nudge for each answer is what it **should** have been minus what it **guessed**.

<NudgeReceipt
  model={d.model}
  sentence={sky}
  caption="Made-up starting dials, real arithmetic. The step size is 0.01 here."
/>

That one subtraction says which way to turn (the sign) and how far (the size). The step size is picked by a person, not by training. Too small and it crawls. Too big and it overshoots.

Should minus guessed is also the slope of the wrongness for that dial. Turn the sky to blue dial up a hair and the surprise score drops, at a rate set by that 59. So the sign points downhill, and the size says how steep it is right here. Nudging every dial by its own should minus guessed, over and over, has a name. It's **gradient descent**.

## Scaling up

A real model runs the same rule. A few things get bigger.

- Its clues aren't single words. They're its whole worked-over reading of the text so far, after many layers (part 1's layers). That's the thousands of directions from the ladder.
- Its answers are every token it knows, over 100,000 of them in newer models.
- Its dials sit in every layer, not only at the end. So the should minus guessed numbers at the end get passed backward through the layers, and every dial gets its own which way and how far. People call that **backpropagation**.
- It doesn't nudge after every guess. It makes a batch of millions of guesses, averages their nudges, then nudges once.
