Part 1 said every wrong guess nudges the dials. When I tried to explain what a nudge is, I got stuck. That's the gap this post fills. No formulas and no symbols. If you can count stairs, you're fine.

## A dial is a slope

A dial is how hard one thing pushes another. For the bean plant, the one thing is the week and the other is the height. Picture it as stairs. How far up you go for each step over is the **slope**, and most dials are just that.

<StairSlope />

A spot on a line works like a receipt. Start somewhere, then add the slope once for every step.

<GrowthReceipt />

So this line has two dials, the slope and the start height. Training's job is to set them.

## The miss

Here's training by hand. You get the dots, and your job is the line. The gap between a dot and the line is a **miss**.

Adding the misses up as they are doesn't work, though. Under and over cancel out.

<MissMath />

One look at the dots plus one nudge is a **round**. Your turn.

<FitLine
  beans={d.beans}
  caption="Made-up bean data. The total miss is each miss multiplied by itself, added up. Let it learn runs real training on these eight dots."
/>

Notice what the computer never gets. Nobody hands it the right line. It gets the dots and how far off it is at each one, and it nudges.

## Downhill

How does it know which way to nudge? Draw the total miss for every setting of the slope dial and you get a valley.

<MissHill
  beans={d.beans}
  start={d.hill.start}
  frames={d.hill.frames}
  feel={d.hill.feel}
  caption="Only the slope dial moves here. The start height stays at its best value, so the bottom of this valley is the line you just watched training find. The beans are made up, so these misses are too."
/>

Each step is the step size times how steep the ground is under the ball. Steep ground, big step. Flat ground, tiny step. The step size is something a person picks, not training, and picking it is fussy.

<StepSize
  beans={d.beans}
  start={d.hill.start}
  rows={d.hill.steps}
  caption="Same valley, same start, three step sizes. The values are made up."
/>

With both dials, the valley becomes a bowl. The computer is a blindfolded hiker, feeling for downhill with its feet. Doing that for every dial at once is called **gradient descent**.

<MissBowl />

## From one line to billions of dials

A real model plays the same game with far more dials.

<SameGame rows={d.same} />

(A real model is also more than one straight line. It stacks many, with bends between them, so it can fit curves. That's a post of its own.)

Each guess is part 1's game. Hide the next word, guess, and score the miss.

Then do that trillions of times.

<TrainingReceipt />

Trillions of guesses, and every so often a small nudge downhill for billions of dials. That's why it takes weeks to months on thousands of chips.
