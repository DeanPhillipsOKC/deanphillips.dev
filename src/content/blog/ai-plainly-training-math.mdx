Part 1 said every wrong guess nudges the dials. When I tried to explain what a nudge is, I got stuck. That's the gap this post fills. No formulas and no symbols. If you can count stairs, you're fine.

## A dial is a slope

A dial is how hard one thing pushes another. Picture it as stairs. How far up you go for each step over is the **slope**, and that's all a dial is.

<StairSlope />

A spot on a line works like a receipt. Start somewhere, then add the slope once for every step.

<GrowthReceipt />

So this line has two dials, the tilt and the start height. Training's job is to set them.

## The miss

Here's training by hand. You get the dots, and your job is the line. The gap between a dot and the line is a **miss**.

Adding the misses up as they are doesn't work, though. Under and over cancel out.

<MissMath />

Your turn.

<FitLine
  beans={d.beans}
  caption="Made-up bean data. The total miss is each miss multiplied by itself, added up. Let it learn runs real training on these eight dots."
/>

Notice what the computer never gets. Nobody hands it the right line. It gets the dots and its total miss, and it nudges. (One look at the dots plus one nudge is a **round**.)

## Downhill

How does it know which way to nudge? Draw the total miss for every setting of the tilt dial and you get a valley.

<MissHill
  beans={d.beans}

  caption="Only the tilt dial moves here. The start height stays at its best value, so the bottom of this valley is the line you just watched training find. Numbers made up."

Each step is the step size times the slope under the ball. Steep ground, big step. Flat ground, tiny step. The step size is the one thing a person picks, and picking it is fussy.

<StepSize

With both dials, the valley becomes a bowl. You're the hiker now, blindfolded, feeling for downhill with your feet. Doing that for every dial at once is called **gradient descent**.

<MissBowl />

## From one line to billions of dials

A real model is the same move with more dials. It has many layers of pushes, with a small bend between them. The bend matters more than it sounds.

<BendStack

/>

Each guess is part 1's game. Hide the next word, guess, and score the miss.

Then do that a few trillion times.

Trillions of guesses, in batches, each batch nudging billions of dials a little downhill. That's why it takes weeks to months on thousands of chips.
