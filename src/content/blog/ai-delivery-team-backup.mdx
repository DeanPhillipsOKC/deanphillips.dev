This is part 7 of a series about the AI agents that build RepBunny, a strength training app I work on around a full-time job. The [overview](/blog/ai-delivery-team/) covers the whole setup. This part is about one feature, and the reviewer earning its keep on it.

## The catch in the privacy promise

RepBunny has no accounts and no servers holding anyone's data. Your workouts stay on the phone you logged them on. That's what makes it safe to let AI agents do most of the typing.

It has a cost, though. If we never have your data, we can't give it back. Lose the phone, reset it, or delete the app, and months of workouts are gone. The app already had a manual Back up button. A button you have to remember to tap isn't much of a backup.

So on October 13 I decided the app should keep a copy for you, in a folder you control, like your own iCloud Drive. The file goes to your folder, never to us. On October 23 I added two asks, after I couldn't find where my own backups went. Say plainly which folder the copy is in, and make restoring it easy.

On the morning of October 27 I moved it ahead of everything else. Losing someone's history is the worst thing this app can do, so it ships before launch.

## An hour from merge to the bug that mattered

I built the first version with an agent in a session on my laptop. After every finished workout, the app saves a copy to the folder you picked, plus a dated copy once a week, keeping the newest four. The full end-to-end suite didn't finish on the laptop (it ran out of memory), so I handed the branch to a lane to run the gate and open the pull request.

The change touched native code on both phones and the file where my product decisions live, so it was in the highest risk tier. That means the [review agent](/blog/ai-delivery-team-review/) reads it, and then it waits for my approval. The review took three minutes. It found no blockers and three should-fix items. The first one was the one that mattered.

I approved it with the bug in it. No release had the change yet, and the bug had its own issue to fix before launch.

The fix is small. The first save to a newly picked folder checks for an existing copy. If one is there, it's kept as "RepBunny backup kept" plus the date, and the weekly cleanup never touches that name. The test seeds a folder with three workouts, picks it on a phone with one, and checks the three-workout copy is still there.

None of this showed up in a passing test, because nobody had written that test yet. The reviewer found it by reading the diff and asking who the feature was for.

## Then I changed my mind

About ten minutes after approving it, I filed a new issue that undid part of it. The first version asked on the screen after your first workout, "Want to keep a copy?" Someone leaving the gym is going to tap Not now. A backup that depends on a decision is one most people won't have.

So the copy is now on by default, in a folder the app picks.

One part of that only I could do. iCloud storage for the app has to be switched on in Apple's developer portal, and that's my account, not an agent's. It went on my list as a task, I did it in about seven minutes, and the build could go ahead. That version merged at 1:14 PM.

The other two should-fix items were folded into it. One was my October 23 ask about naming the folder, which the first version had missed. The other was the privacy policy, which now describes the copy.

From "this ships before launch" to on by default took about three and a half hours on a Tuesday. The checks only a real phone can do (saving to iCloud, restoring on a fresh install) are queued for my next test batch, like every other native change.

## What broke

- **The laptop couldn't run the gate.** It ran out of memory. The rule for that is a hand-off to a lane, not a skipped gate.
- **The reviewer couldn't merge, again.** It passed the fix for #871 with no findings, then its own session refused the merge call. That's the same safety check that tripped it in [part 5](/blog/ai-delivery-team-review/). This time it said so on the issue right away, and I merged it by hand about 20 minutes later.
- **The first version missed my own ask.** The October 23 comment about naming the folder was on the issue the whole time. The reviewer caught it. The session that built it didn't.

Turns out the best backup button is the one you never see.
