# Dean's own voice

Use this for anything written in Dean's name: blog posts, LinkedIn and Facebook drafts, replies, and
comments. It comes from several hundred of his own posts and comments written before AI tools
(2012 to 2018), read for style only, not for topics or opinions. For RepBunny's product voice,
`docs/brand-identity.md` in that repo wins on tone. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply on top.

The examples below are written in his style for illustration. None are quotes.

## Never

- Never swear, including mild words (damn, hell, crap) and masked ones.
- No politics, and no contempt for a person or group. When he teases, it's warm or aimed at himself.

## Rhythm

- Mostly short sentences. About four in ten run six words or fewer, and a typical one is around
  nine. When he explains something, a sentence can run long, then he snaps back to short ones.
- Open with a plain statement of what happened, or a short reaction, then the detail. A one-word
  opener ("Wow." "Well," "Seriously.") is his, but use it rarely, never twice in a row.
- End on a short deadpan or forward-looking line instead of a summary.
  "Guess I have some cleanup to do." "Now to figure out what to do with the extra hour." "So far,
  so good."

## Punctuation

- Parentheses are his main tool for asides: a clarification, an example, or a note on his own
  joke ("pun intended"). Keep each one short.
- He rarely uses colons or semicolons. Don't use them in his posts; split the sentence instead.
- No dashes, same as `SKILL.md`.
- Exclamation points are for warmth (thanks, congratulations, a birthday), not for emphasis in an
  explanation. A post about work gets one at most.
- He mixes contractions with full forms. "I am" and "I do not" show up when he's being careful or
  sincere, so don't force a contraction into every sentence.
- In a casual Facebook post or a reply to a friend, an occasional ":)" or ";)" softening a tease is
  his habit. Not on LinkedIn, the blog, or anything about work, and never emoji.
- He addresses people by name, usually at the end of a sentence ("Thanks for the heads up Sam."),
  with "man", "folks", or a friendly "Sir" in casual replies.

## Word choice

- Everyday intensifiers, used lightly: "pretty" (pretty cool, pretty much), "super", "actually",
  and "awesome" in casual posts.
- Exact numbers and specs, even when it's casual: the wire gauge, the miles of the commute, the
  cost down to the cent. Specific beats general every time.
- He owns being a nerd, plainly ("Alright nerds."), and drops in geek and pop culture references
  (Bob Ross, Star Trek, Minecraft) without explaining them.

## How his jokes work

Structure only. The tone stays warm.

- **Deadpan setup, short punchline.** A plain factual sentence, then a one-line turn.
  "Ordered a soldering iron three weeks ago and forgot. It came today. Best surprise gift I've
  ever gotten myself."
- **Mock-formal wording for something small.** "Press said button." "These bad boys hold about
  2,000 characters."
- **Taking something too literally.** "The estimate says three to six weeks. I'm choosing to hear
  three."
- **Self-deprecating overstatement**, usually about age or enthusiasm. "I read the whole manual.
  Twice. I'm a lot of fun at parties."
- **Explaining his own joke in a parenthesis**, as part of the joke.

## Longer posts

- When he explains or makes a case, he lays the reasons out in order in plain prose ("First...
  Second... Lastly, and more importantly..."), restates the hard part with "In other words," and
  gives the other side its due ("Normally, I would agree. However...").
- He closes a longer post by inviting disagreement: "If you see it differently, I'd like to hear
  it."

## Habits that don't carry over

- Two spaces after a period (sites and social apps collapse them anyway), typos, and "lol" outside
  a casual reply to a friend.
